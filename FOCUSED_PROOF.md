# Focused extension of the verified DEST repository

This extension is deliberately separate from the proposed equivalence with second-order arithmetic. It has three mathematical targets only. The original ten verified Lean modules are unchanged; their hashes are taken from the supplied successful verification evidence.

## 1. Original DEST, without an axiom of Infinity

Write m for first membership, r for second membership, U(a) for agreement of the two extensions. Use first Extensionality, second Extensionality, unrestricted second-extension comprehension for first-language formulas, and the corrected Uniformity schema. The only induction assumed is the following precise reading of the original natural-number induction scheme (with arbitrary parameters and both relations allowed in the induction formula):

* A zero z has no first members. S(a,b) means that the first members of b are a and the first members of a.
* I(z,t) means that z is a first member of t and each first member of t has a successor in t.
* N(z,a) means that a is a first member of every I(z,t). This predicate does not presume existence of an inductive set.
* For each formula P, if P(z) and for all a,b, N(z,a) and S(a,b) and P(a) imply P(b), then P holds of every N(z,a). This is conditional on z being zero, not on an inductive set existing.

The independent semantic specification `DESTOriginal.Original` contains exactly these schemes. `OriginalSpecification.lean` contains no Infinity field. The previous `ReferenceTheory.lean` remains intact as a historical, independently verified specification; its Infinity axiom is NOT an input to the following derivation.

Uniform collection lemma: if a first-language formula with uniform parameters defines a collection all of whose prospective elements are uniform, its second-extension representative c must be uniform. Otherwise Uniformity forces c to satisfy the formula, hence to be uniform, contradiction. Its first extension then agrees with the formula as well.

Apply this to the false formula to get a uniform empty set z. If a and all its first members are uniform, apply the lemma to `y=a or y m a` to obtain a successor b with the same property. First Extensionality gives uniqueness of that successor. Induction, using the explicit formula `allUniformF`, establishes that every N(z,a) is uniform and has only uniform first members. The parameter z is uniform, and N(z,a) is a first-language formula. Uniform collection produces w with first extension exactly N(z,-). Zero belongs to w. For a in w, the successor constructed above lies in every inductive set, by the definition of inductiveness and first Extensionality. Thus it lies in w. Therefore w is inductive and contained in every inductive set. It is the least inductive set, and w and all its members are uniform. No Infinity, SU, TI, Foundation or standardness of the internal naturals was assumed.

Lean target: `DESTOriginal.exists_uniform_omega`. The bridge `DESTOriginal.original_of_reference` and `DESTOriginal.exists_original_model` show that the earlier verified model also models the Infinity-free specification. The derivation of omega is in arbitrary models of the new specification, not just in the earlier model.

## 2. Add only first-extension Subset Uniformity

SU1 says U(a) and first-extension inclusion b⊆a imply U(b). It says nothing directly about second-extension inclusion. TI1 is the full first-membership set induction schema, with both relations permitted in formulas.

### Mathematical upper bound in ZF

We establish finite-fragment satisfiability relative to ZF, not a single uniform definable interpretation of the entire theory in ZF. Work first in ZFC. Fix a finite fragment of Original+SU1+TI1. Only finitely many first-language formulas φ(y,p) occur in its Uniformity instances. For each include in a finite reflection family φ, the formula Rφ(a,p) saying ∀y(y∈a ↔ φ(y,p)), and ∃a Rφ(a,p). Include all subformulas needed for reflection. By finite Lévy–Montague reflection choose limit ordinals ω<α<β so that Vα and Vβ agree on that family for parameters in Vα. Take a countable elementary substructure M of (Vβ,∈,Vα), containing ω and every numeral, and designate U=M∩Vα. Here the unary predicate Vα is included in the elementary structure, not in the DEST first language.

Let E(a)={x∈M:x∈a}. It is injective by elementarity. Let D be all first-language definable subsets of M with arbitrary parameters (a countable set). Initially assign F(a)=E(a) for a∈U. Call an extension protected if it is defined by one of the finitely many relevant Uniformity formulas with parameters in U. A protected extension D0 comes from the corresponding ambient extension D* in Vβ. If D* has a representative in Vβ, reflection places a representative in Vα, and elementarity places it in M∩Vα. Thus D0 already has its fixed representative. Conversely, if D0 has a representative in M, elementarity ensures a corresponding ambient representative in Vβ; it is fixed for the same reason. If D* is unrepresented, its ranks are unbounded in β: any subset of Vβ of bounded rank belongs to Vβ because β is limit. In this case D* has infinitely many points outside Vα, and by elementarity D0 has infinitely many points outside U.

Complete the assignment by the countable matching lemma already verified in the baseline. For each protected unfixed extension choose a fresh point of that extension outside U, ensuring self-membership in its second extension. For unprotected extensions choose any fresh outside point whose first trace differs from that extension. Such points exist because M\U is infinite and first traces are injective. In the reverse direction use fresh singleton extensions of outside points. Such singletons have an ambient representative, and cannot be protected: a fixed representative would lie in Vα, which is transitive, forcing its unique member into U. They are available in D and are not fixed. Exclude the finite list of used extensions and at most one extension equal to the chosen point's first trace. The alternating matching extends the fixed assignments to an injection onto D and fixes exactly U. Define r(y,a) iff y∈F(a).

Both Extensionalities and all Comprehension instances hold. Every requested Uniformity instance holds by protection. If a∈U and the first trace of b is included in that of a, elementarity implies actual subsethood in Vβ, and rank downward closure implies b∈Vα. Thus b∈U: SU1 holds. The first relation is well-founded, giving all instances of TI1; the included numerals and ω give the relational natural-number scheme as well. This proves each fixed finite fragment satisfiable. In ZF one can apply the same fixed-fragment argument inside L (which satisfies ZFC). Compactness, in the external metatheory, gives Con(ZF) ⇒ Con(Original+SU1+TI1). No inaccessible cardinal is assumed. This does not assert ZF internally proves the simultaneous satisfiability of all fragments or a single uniform interpretation.

### Lean construction

`RankCut.lean` uses Mathlib's ZFSet ranks and small indexed ranges to form a rank cut by closing a succession of rank bounds under the witness queries from the original Hull module. The cut is a represented set and downward closed under first subsethood and membership. `RankModel.lean` reuses `DEST.countable_matching` and the original hull; its uniform domain is the intersection of the new rank cut with that countable hull. The only changes in the matching argument concern representation and freshness relative to the rank cut. Its unconditional semantic model theorem is `DESTRank.exists_SU_TI_model`. The mathematical finite-fragment reduction above is the reason for the ZF upper bound; the Lean theorem is not an arithmetization of ZF provability or a formal proof transformation inside ZF. In particular the full ZFSet satisfaction function used by Lean is not asserted to be definable in ZF's universe.

## 3. A hereditarily uniform set model of Zermelo, from Original+SU1 only

A transitive uniform set W all of whose first members are uniform is hereditarily uniform in the stated sense: its least transitive superset (with W included as a subset) is W itself. More generally one may use a transitive uniform-element witness; intersection of all transitive supersets is uniform by uniform collection and is the least witness.

Uniform power: the formula y⊆a with uniform parameter a defines only uniform objects by SU1. Uniform collection therefore produces its uniform powerset b, and all first members of b are uniform. This proves Uniform Power without adding it as an axiom. Uniform pairing, union when all prospective members are uniform, intersections, Kuratowski ordered pairs, and adjoining an object to a uniform set of uniform objects all follow by uniform collection.

Let w be the least inductive set derived in Section 1. Define a stage by an internally coded graph. A graph g records pairs (k,a) whose first coordinates are members of w. Every recorded value at zero is w. Each recorded value at a nonzero k must have a recorded predecessor j with S(j,k) and must be the powerset of its value at j. This downward-closed graph condition uses only first membership. A graph need not be assumed externally finite or standard; it is sufficient to show that each internal k has a graph recording its value. The base graph is a singleton, and a successor graph adjoins one new pair (k+1,P(a)) to a previous uniform graph of uniform pairs. Induction is over the formula asserting the existence of this graph with uniformity properties. Therefore every internal index has a stage. Induction also proves that every possible recorded value is uniform with uniform elements and is transitive; uniqueness follows by successor injectivity on the von Neumann naturals and Extensionality for powersets. Numeric comparison and the nesting of stages follow by the same internal induction. Thus this is precisely the internal sequence A0=w, A_(k+1)=P(A_k). No sequence existing as a set is postulated.

Well-foundedness: w is transitive, all its members are transitive and irreflexive, by natural induction. Every nonempty subset b of w has a minimal element: if not, induction on k in w gives k∉b and all first members of k outside b. This contradicts nonemptiness. If a is transitive and well-founded, P(a) is transitive and well-founded. For a nonempty subset d of P(a), if d meets a, separate d∩a and find a minimal member there; transitivity makes it minimal in d. Otherwise every member of d has all its members in a, so any member of d is minimal. The intersection exists because d is uniform by SU1 and a is uniform with uniform members. Induction on the stages gives their well-foundedness. No ambient set induction is used.

The first-language formula ∃k,a (Stage(z,w,k,a) and y∈a) has only uniform parameters and uniform prospective members. Uniform collection gives an actual set W with that first extension. It is transitive, uniform, and all its members are uniform. Its transitive closure is itself. W contains w and zero; it contains every stage as an element of the next one. It is closed under every existing subset of each of its elements (a subset of an element of A_k belongs to A_(k+1)). Any two elements belong to a common stage by comparison and nesting. This yields pairing and union in W; the powerset of an element of A_k belongs to A_(k+2). Infinity and Extensionality follow from transitivity and the presence of w. Foundation follows by placing a nonempty element of W inside a well-founded stage.

For unrestricted Separation, let p be any first-order formula in the language of Z. Relativize all its quantifiers to W. This is still a first-language formula of DEST, and all parameters, including W, are uniform. For a∈W, the formula y∈a and p^W(y,params) defines only uniform objects. Uniform collection gives its first extension b, and b⊆a implies b∈W. This establishes every instance of full Separation, not just bounded Separation. The Lean code explicitly translates all permitted first-order formulas and proves semantic preservation. Z here is ordinary Zermelo with Foundation and Infinity, without Choice or Replacement.

Lean target: `DESTOriginal.exists_hereditarily_uniform_Z_model`. It assumes only `Original m r` and `SU1 m r` and produces one object W in the model, not an externally chosen family of domains. Its specification proves all Zermelo axioms semantically, including a quantification over every permitted formula for Separation. It does not assert an internal arithmetized satisfaction predicate or the separate syntactic sentence Con(Z).

For the stronger internal coded model assertion, the same mathematical construction supports a satisfaction relation: finite assignments into W are internally finite graphs, bounded by P(ω×W); formula and assignment codes form a hereditarily uniform domain. For each internal complexity level the Tarski recursion clauses are first-language formulas with uniform parameters and define collections of uniform coded pairs. Uniform collection gives each partial satisfaction table, and internal natural induction gives finite sequences of those tables, coded as graphs just as above. Uniqueness follows from induction on complexity; collecting pairs occurring in the tables gives the uniform satisfaction relation. For an arbitrary internal formula code its satisfaction slice on a∈W can be separated using that relation as a uniform parameter; closure of W under subsets puts the slice in W. This supplies the mathematical route to the internally coded model assertion. The Lean target in this revision is the semantic scheme, not a separate verification of this arithmetization.
